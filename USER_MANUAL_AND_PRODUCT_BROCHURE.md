# High Voltage NCVD
## Non-Contact High Voltage Proximity Detector
### With Bluetooth 5.0 & Remote Mobile Monitoring

---

## 1. What is It?

**High Voltage NCVD** is an industrial-grade, non-contact proximity safety detector used by utility professionals, linesmen, and substation engineers to determine whether overhead lines, busbars, and switchyard apparatus are **LIVE** or **DEAD** from a safe distance, well beyond the flashover arcing zone.

It provides **2-in-1 Dual Functionality**:
1. **Live AC Voltage Detection**: Warns of live operating AC voltages from **230 V up to 765 kV AC**.
2. **Hazardous Induced Voltage Detection**

---

## 2. Key Features & Benefits

* **Wide Selectable Voltage Coverage**: 12 dedicated rotary positions covering Low Voltage (230V/440V) to Ultra High Voltage (765kV).
* **Dual-Action Alarms**: High-intensity flashing **Red LEDs** (One LED for Live Line, One LED for Mode) + **Loud Sound Alarm**.
* **Automatic Built-In Self-Test**: Automatically checks the device health every time you power on.
* **Bluetooth Remote Monitoring with app**.
* **Universal Sunrise Spline Fitting**: Standard sunrise connector attaches securely to all standard utility telescopic fiberglass (FRP) hotsticks.

## 3. How to Use (Step-by-Step)

```
[1. Mount to Hotstick] ──► [2. Turn ON & Self-Test] ──► [3. Select Voltage] ──► [4. Approach Line]
```

1. **Mount to Insulating Hotstick**:
   Attach the detector head to an approved fiberglass hotstick using the universal sunrise spline fitting. Tighten the wing-nut firmly at the desired working angle.
2. **Turn Power ON & Listen for Self-Test**:
   Slide the power switch to **ON**. The detector will sound a **single confirmation beep** and illuminate both Red LEDs. This confirms the device is healthy and ready for live working.
3. **Select System Voltage**:
   Rotate the knurled selector knob to the setting matching the nominal voltage of the circuit under test (e.g., `11kV`, `33kV`, `132kV`, `220kV`, `400kV`, `765kV`).
4. **Approach the Conductor**:
   Slowly advance the detector toward the target conductor up to the recommended safe standoff distance:
   * 🔴 **Continuous Loud Alarm + Solid Live Red LED** = **LIVE LINE (DANGER)**. Withdraw immediately!
   * 🟢 **Silent + Live LED OFF** = **LINE IS DEAD (SAFE)**.

---

## 4. How to Detect Induced Voltage on Dead Lines

When a transmission line is de-energized, adjacent energized circuits frequently induce dangerous voltages onto not properly grounded lines:

1. **Verify De-Energized State**:
   With the selector switch at nominal line voltage (e.g., `220kV`), approach the line. If the device remains silent, the line is de-energized.
2. **Perform Step-Down Sweep**:
   Step the rotary selector down one range at a time (`132kV` $\rightarrow$ `66kV` $\rightarrow$ `33kV` $\rightarrow$ `11kV` $\rightarrow$ `1.1kV`). Each lower range increases input sensitivity.
3. **Identify Induced Potential**:
   When the selector reaches the range corresponding to the induced potential, the detector sounds a **pulsing 2 Hz chirp** accompanied by a **flashing Red LED** (`INDUCED VOLTAGE`).
